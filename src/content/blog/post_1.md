We could start at many points in this story, but I think it's most appropiate that we start at the moment where it matters most, when I made my last portfolio. My last portfolio was made a little more than a year ago in 2025, and it's very serviceable! I am still very proud of it and I don't think it's obsolete or anything. But there are still a few important issues with it, ones that I didn't want to repeat on this version.

### List of issues

- **To the untrained eye, the portfolio looked very good**, and in fact fit very well with the industry standard for a dev portfolio, much more than this current version. But that was kind of the issue! I was too focused on imitating what I saw on other sites that I ended up just making a good but still amateur attempt that didn't stand out from the crowd. It's even worse now that vibe-coding a portfolio in Claude Code could probably give you a similar looking page in minutes.

- **The repo was a fork from another user.** I know, i know, what was I thinking using somebody's template for my own portfolio? But in my defense, if you look at the original template I used and the portfolio itself, they look completely different. I remember doing the fork because I found the animations cool and it had a built-in email tool, and I wasn't smart enough at that time to put them on my own project instead.

- **It was filled to the brim with walls of text I barely even needed.** I think I was too focused on explaining who I was that I forgot that much of what people are looking for your portfolio is for you to **show, not tell.**

- **And most importantly, it didn't really bring me much results.** To its defense, I wasn't quite looking for jobs back when I deployed it, I barely had even written my CV by that point and my LinkedIn was just there for the sake of being there. But even still, I feel like it doesn't quite represent what I actually could bring to the job market. If you had to pick this one out of a crowd and say which one is mine, you'd probably have a hard time if my name wasn't in bold at the top.

### Initial goals

Okay, so we've got all the issues noted, now what's the plan? Well my goals from the beginning were these:

1. **This time around, I wanted to highlight one main thing: that I'm not replaceable.** I wanted to make a website that communicated my taste, my eye for detail, my passion, the human qualities that an AI could try to imitate, but wouldn't be able to replicate correctly without my lived experience. I wanted it to be off the beaten path, but still not _too_ unconventional.

2. **Second of all, I wanted it to actually be built from the ground up this time.** Even if the original one only retained 2% of the original code, going on GitHub and seeing the fork icon of _shame_ pushed me to do a portfolio again from scratch. That way no one can make any claims about my work.

3. **Third of all, I wanted to be more intentional with the copywriting.** I don't claim I'm an amazing writer, but I felt like it was a bit of a shame how long yet generic a lot of the text on the original was. So this time I felt like it was more appropiate to keep the portfolio itself brief, and add a blog section where I could ramble more freely, like this! Any time I have tried to ask an AI for advice on copywriting, the result was so off-putting that I decided to just stick to my gut and do my own thing.

4. **And finally the last one, I wanted to actually describe the job I wanted in the first place much more accurately, so the right people could finally find me.** This one was the last one of the bunch to decide, because for a long time I felt like the industry was funnelling all devs towards becoming full-stack developers, which don't get me wrong, to some extent I do consider myself qualified a full-stack developer, but I didn't think that full-stack was the best title for describing me and the work that I was trying to find. And then I found out about the concept of Design Engineers, and it made everything click! That's exactly how I thought of myself in my head, but seing it actually labelled made me feel much more invigorated to do the project.

## Initial concepts and failed attempts

One of my first ideas for where to get inspiration was actually [neocities.org](https://neocities.org)! For anybody that doesn't know about neocities, it's basically a hosting platform and community that is based around making little personal websites, often inspired by the individuality and simplicity of old 90s/2000s blog posts and other websites. I came in looking for inspiration there because I thought I could possibly mash the techniques I learned while making run-of-the-mill modern websites with a more vintage look in order to make an interesting mix! And from that came three main attempts, one dates back a few months, and two are way more recent, just a few weeks ago from now.

### Miku Portfolio

The first one was the most unconventional one, it was an attempt to recreate the "Project DIVA" UI with my own twist (the twist being a different background, boring text, and no Miku, so essentially a downgrade). It's funny because you _really_ just have to trust your gut, sometimes an idea can feel amazing for two seconds but at some point your mind subconsciously realizes there's not much more to mine out of it, yet it didn't stop me from trying. I probably should've gone back to the drawing board and reflect before trying to keep improving it, there were a lot of points where I knew it wasn't working out but I was just stalling and trying to force it.

### Hand-drawn Portfolio

This one was very short-lived, which in this case, was a good thing. A lot of the influence for what I wanted to do with this iteration came from the idea of making a website that felt almost "analog". Center around bringing texture to the forefront and making stuff look "handcrafted". I thought it would be a nice poetic way to contrast off of the cold nature of most websites and content right now in the age of AI.

### The iOS7 Portfolio

When I realized that the second attempt was not really working out, I dropped the project, and decided to get my mind off of it for a while, so I could revisit it with a fresh pair of eyes. After enough time had passed. I then went back to the brainstorming phase, and this time I *really* wanted to focus on looking at the core values I wanted to communicate, and base the general vibe from there. 

I started to think about aestethics less like a shell you put on top of a website, and more so a reflection of what the website is trying to communicate. So on my initial board, I didn't put any pictures up, instead I put key words I wanted the website to communicate, and then slowly over time I made a collection of pictures and references that represented the feeling I wanted the portfolio to have.

I then, started to look for thematic references, that aligned with these words. These references included everything from musical rollouts, product branding, operating systems, etc. Stuff that in some way shape or form connected to one or more words on the board and brought up similar feelings. The important thing was that *all* of these references needed to have some sort of connection with the spirit of the project, anything that was just there for the vibes needed to be discarded.

  <img src="/blog/post_1/image_6.webp" alt="Pinterest board with all my references including black and white minimalist pictures, photos of celebrities, the daisy chain tour, among others">
  <figcaption>Pinterest board with all my aestethic/thematic references including black and white minimalist pictures, photos of celebrities, video game and phone operating systems, among others</figcaption>
</figure>

One of the main things on the list that kept resonating with me was iOS 7. Because it was this very controversial update, yet it was almost inevitable to not get obsessed with it at the time. It was the talk of the town, and the way it totally changed the design landscape is something that has been studied for years. I liked this idea of "too good to be ignored", and projects that forced the mainstream to "align to them". I'm fascinated with people who know just the right balance between appealing to the masses and also keeping them on their toes just enough.

But I think I sort of went a little too literal with the iOS inspiration for the first iterations because I just started to recreate the macOS dock layout :P. It was ironic because I was so tired of seeing OS portfolios everywhere yet I didn't realize until later I almost did it myself lol. It just felt like a surface read on what I enjoyed from my references instead of a meaningful reinterpretation. For curiosity's sake though I also tried doing the opposite, and going for a skeumorphic dock and app icons, but it was just the same problem in a different font. It was still very useful for me to get accustomed to using the tool I would use for this project which was [GSAP](https://gsap.com/). The only thing that made it all the way from here into the finished version is the light weight version of Geist I used for some of the text, because I found out it looks really pretty and a little like the Apple font from those iOS7 era advertisements.

There was also a secondary motif that I wanted to represent on this version of the portfolio, and you'll see it pop up a little with the upcoming project too. It was this idea of cultural osmosis, of how big of a role others have on our own personal style, and I wanted to find a way to somehow highlight and put my references front and center instead of hiding them. Highlighting the fact that I'm a "student of the world" in a sense, taking bits and pieces out of everything that interests me and turning it into something new.

## The playground

At this point, I decided I wanted to play with code again, because I had spent a long time ruminating with the core ideas of the project, and I wanted the website to have a hook that actually felt different from the rest without feeling too gimmicky. So I started ideating, and one of the first ideas that stuck was the heat map. I had this vision of this black and white page where the person would run the mouse through the website and the mouse would invert the colors with a blurry overlay set to difference. And there would actually be an API that accumulated the most common mouse spots from all users and it turned into a heat map where you could see mouse activity overall.

I started coding this idea on a playground repository where I could toy with it. First I told the AI to give me the rough algorithm to make it work, and then I started manipulating the configurations, changing the way the heat map looked. Whether it was the shape, the size, the blurryness (or lack thereof), the speed, until I got to the most satisfying version...

  <img src="/blog/post_1/image_7.webp" alt="A heat map in the form of a heart-shape on top of a black and white website">

But it wasn't good enough for me... the concept was cool, but in execution it means you have a lot of ugly scribbles on your page that could sometimes mess with the readability of things. Still, this introduced two main ideas that were key in developing the final version:

- Restricting myself to _only_ black and white in order to bring maximum contrast.

- The idea of making algorithms for the animations, and then fidgeting around with the configurations to find all the possible combinations that arise.

And that's when an idea struck to me: "What if I did this lo-fi screen, with a perlin noise grid, that would slowly replace the cells line by line like an old screen?" So I made another branch and inmediately started working on it. At first I tried with teeny tiny images, since it was a potential way to revisit the cultural osmosis concept mentioned earlier, but it didn't really look the way I wanted it too, even after countless amounts of tinkering. By that point, I just concluded that the biggest issue with the "highlighting my references" concept was that by putting them on the home screen, I'm kind of implying that I designed them, which is not the intended effect.

But then I tried with ascii, and that's when I knew, this is _exactly_ where I wanted to go. It was the combinations of all the things I wanted. It was quirky yet sophisticated. It harkens back to old technology but translated to modern design. It's something that an AI wouldn't think of by itself when tasked to do a portfolio. It was a natural conclusion to the long winded quest I went throughout all these different projects.
