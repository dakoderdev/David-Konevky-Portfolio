It was easy deciding what the first opening article of the blog was going to be, but I wasn't quite sure what else I was going to write about right after. There's some ideas I have, specifically for one *big* project that's not out yet, but I'm waiting for when I reach a later stage of development before I begin writing about that specific project.

So in the midst of pondering what else I could write about, and staring at my shiny new portfolio, the shades of black and white reminded me of a totally different project that dates back as far as 2023 and as recently as a few months from now. The thing is... I've always loved design, in all of its forms. But one of my most enduring and stable relationships with the art of design has always been **graphic design**. 

It started like it started for almost everybody. First you scribble stuff as a child, then you find out about tools like MSPaint or Canva and go to town with all the colors and crazy fonts that you can find. And then you start seeing these graphics on the internet that look so cool, and you try so hard to recreate them in these primitive programs that you're familiar with but you can tell that it doesn't look quite right, so you start researching what other tools are out there that could help you get closer to your goal. And you start getting design tutorials for these daunting but enticing pro tools, that seem to have no limit as to what they can build. So you bite the bullet and start trying them out.

And while I have toyed with tools like Illustrator or Photoshop every once in a while, at some point during 2023 I decided I wanted to challenge myself, and that's how I came up with the "Subject Cover Challenge" (not the greatest name for sure but I can't think of a better one). As the name implies, the challenge arose from the fact that I needed to organize my school work better, and I wanted to make unique covers for all of my subjects. So I went "What better way to learn Photoshop and Illustrator than by forcing myself to make all the covers in those programs?", and that's exactly what I did. Here are the limitations I gave myself early on while making these:

- These covers ALL had to be done using black and white, in order to reduce printing costs, and also as a way to test how different I could make all of these look without relying on colors to do that for me.
- As I said before, all of these covers needed to be done with Photoshop or Illustrator, or both. There was no point in doing the challenge if I didn't use these tools (until Figma came in but we'll talk about it later).
- They all have to share the same size which is A4 for printing.
- A rule that wasn't set in stone but I tried abiding by in practice was to force myself to use at least one new technique or filter I hadn't used yet. It wasn't required but most of the covers I made do follow this rule.

### Minor remarks about Batch Nº1
- One of my favorite effects as you can see was threshold. Threshold was a life saver for this challenge, because it made black and white images look much more dramatic and it led to some really pretty effects.
- The bezel tool deserves a mention too. I loved making my designs look 3D and you can see glimpses of it on image 1 and 4. It's one of the things that I wish they added to Figma honestly.
- There's not much use of SVG's but trust that WILL come later.

### Minor remarks about Batch Nº4
- This is the first batch exclusively made in Figma, no other tools this time.
- I also made these around the time Figma dropped shaders, so a LOT of these image effects, like the dither, the metallic text effect or the dotted effect were made using those shaders. They're pretty cool! Hopefully they add more in the future (including bevels & bezels like omg).
- The covers have gotten increasingly lighter in university don't you think? With the exception of Data Analysis.
- Even if these aren't nearly as complex as the ones made with Illustrator & Photoshop, they show that Figma is an amazing tool and hopefully it gets better from here on out.

And that's all batches recapped! It was very fun scouring for these pictures and compiling them into one place because I can see a lot of my evolution on these covers. It's funny because some of my favorite covers were the first ones, but I think I have a soft spot for many of the newer covers because they pushed me out of my comfort zone and try a different approach for them than just → Y2K →  Threshold → done. 

Also it's pretty interesting because you can see the difference in their appearance from when I switched from the Adobe Suite into Figma as a primary tool. As much as I do miss the effects and complexity you can get from things like Photoshop’s effects and tools, Figma wins solely because it's free, much lighter than Illustrator and Photoshop while handling most of what they previously required separate tools for on it's own, and it's amazing specifically for ux/ui development. 

But even then you can still sense the throughline of my own taste and style sensibilities, and I think making all of these in black and white may have made me gravitate toward working with duotones. It’s crazy how much you can tell just by looking at these pictures.
